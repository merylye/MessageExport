Download My Messages

Save a copy of your iPhone texts (iMessage and SMS) onto your computer. You pick which conversations to save and which dates to include for each one.

Before you start

  • A Mac or a Windows computer.
  • Your iPhone and its charging cable, unless your texts already show up in the Messages app on your Mac.
  • About 20 minutes. Backing up an iPhone can take up to an hour.

Your messages stay on your computer

This app reads your messages and saves files on your own computer. It never sends anything over the internet.

Which computer do you have?

I have a MacMacBook, iMac, Mac mini I have a Windows PCDell, HP, Lenovo, Surface, and so on

Steps for Mac

  1. Check whether your texts are already on your Mac

    Open the Messages app on your Mac. Click a conversation and scroll up.

    If you can see messages going back as far as you want, skip to step 3. If you can't, do step 2.

  2. Back up your iPhone to your Mac

    Only if step 1 came up short
    1. Plug your iPhone into your Mac with its cable.
    2. If your iPhone asks whether to trust this computer, tap Trust and enter your iPhone passcode.
    3. Open Finder (the smiling face icon in your Dock) and click your iPhone in the left sidebar.
    4. Under Backups, choose Back up all of the data on your iPhone to this Mac.
    5. Make sure Encrypt local backup is not ticked.
    6. Click Back Up Now and wait until it finishes.

  3. Download the app

    Download for Mac
    1. Open your Downloads folder and double-click the file you downloaded to unzip it.
    2. Drag Download My Messages into your Applications folder.

  4. Open it for the first time

    Your Mac will warn you that it can't check this app, because it wasn't bought through Apple. You only need to get past this once.

    1. Double-click Download My Messages. When the warning appears, click Done.
    2. Click the Apple menu in the top-left corner and open System Settings.
    3. Click Privacy & Security and scroll down to the message about Download My Messages.
    4. Click Open Anyway, then enter your Mac password.

    On older Macs, you can instead right-click the app and choose Open.

  5. Let the app read your messages

    macOS keeps your messages locked away from apps until you say otherwise. The app will show a "One-time setup" box.

    1. Click Open System Settings.
    2. Turn on the switch next to Download My Messages. Enter your Mac password if asked.
    3. If the app isn't in the list, click +, open Applications, and choose it.
    4. Quit the app (press Command and Q together) and open it again.

    If your Mac asks whether the app can see your Contacts, click Allow. That way you'll see names instead of phone numbers.

  6. Pick your conversations and dates

    At the top, next to Read messages from, choose This Mac's Messages app, or your iPhone backup if you made one in step 2.

    1. Tick the box next to each conversation you want. Type a name in the search box to find someone quickly.
    2. In the Date range to export column, pick All time, Last 30 days, Last 6 months, or Last year.
    3. For specific dates, pick Custom and choose a start date and an end date.

  7. Choose what to save

    CSVA spreadsheet you can open in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets.
    JSONA file for people who analyze data with code. Leave it ticked if you're unsure.
    Copy attachmentsTick this to save photos, videos, and other files too. This takes more space. If it's off, the export only notes that a photo or file was there.

  8. Save the files

    1. Click Export… and choose where to save (your Documents folder is fine). Click Export Here.
    2. When it finishes, click Show in Finder.

    You'll find a folder called Messages Export with today's date. Inside is one file per conversation.

Steps for Windows

  1. Install Apple Devices

    Open the Microsoft Store app, search for Apple Devices, and click Get. It's free.

    If you already use iTunes on this PC, you can use iTunes instead.

  2. Back up your iPhone to your PC

    1. Plug your iPhone into your PC with its cable.
    2. If your iPhone asks whether to trust this computer, tap Trust and enter your iPhone passcode.
    3. Open Apple Devices and click your iPhone in the sidebar.
    4. Under Backups, choose Back up all of the data on your iPhone to this PC.
    5. Make sure Encrypt local backup is not ticked.
    6. Click Back Up Now and wait until it finishes. This can take up to an hour.

  3. Download the app

    Download for Windows

    Save the file somewhere you'll find it, such as your Desktop.

  4. Open it for the first time

    Double-click Download My Messages. Windows may show a blue box that says "Windows protected your PC", because the app is new and unknown to Microsoft.

    1. Click More info.
    2. Click Run anyway.

    After a few seconds, your web browser opens a page called Download My Messages. The page is running on your own computer, not on a website.

    If no page appears after 15 seconds, double-click the app again.

  5. Pick your conversations and dates

    Next to Read from, choose your iPhone backup. It's named after your phone.

    1. Tick the box next to each conversation you want. Type a name in the search box to find someone quickly.
    2. In the Date range to export column, pick All time, Last 30 days, Last 6 months, or Last year.
    3. For specific dates, pick Custom and choose a start date and an end date.

  6. Choose what to save

    CSVA spreadsheet you can open in Excel or Google Sheets.
    JSONA file for people who analyze data with code. Leave it ticked if you're unsure.
    Copy attachmentsTick this to save photos, videos, and other files too. This takes more space. If it's off, the export only notes that a photo or file was there.

  7. Save the files

    1. Next to Save to, the page shows your Documents folder. Click Browse… to pick a different folder.
    2. Click Export. When it finishes, click Show export folder.
    3. Click Quit in the top-right corner of the page, then close the browser tab.

    You'll find a folder called Messages Export with today's date. Inside is one file per conversation.

If something goes wrong

Some conversations or older messages are missing

The app can only save what's on the computer. On a Mac, open Messages, go to Settings, then iMessage, and make sure Enable Messages in iCloud is on, then give it time to download. Otherwise, back up your iPhone (Mac step 2) and choose the backup in the app.

It says my backup is encrypted

Turn off "Encrypt local backup" in Finder (Mac) or Apple Devices (Windows). It will ask for the backup password you set when you turned it on. Then click Back Up Now again.

Photos are marked as "missing"

Some photos are stored only in iCloud and never downloaded to the computer. The export lists them so you know they existed.

I see phone numbers instead of names

On a Mac, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Contacts, and turn on Download My Messages. When you use an iPhone backup, names come from the contacts saved on the phone.

That's it. Your messages are saved.
